# Sheng et al. (2026) Study 5c: leadership-effectiveness items (Table S10.1)

These are the 57 descriptions that Sample E (China, T2) rated in Sheng, Andrews & van Vugt (2026, *JAP*), Study 5.

**Source.** Xiaotian Sheng's PhD thesis, *Measuring the Psychology of Followership* (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, 2026; https://doi.org/10.5463/thesis.1652; CC BY-ND), Supplemental Materials **S10, Table S10.1**, thesis pp. 267-269 (PDF pp. 275-277). The thesis calls this "Study 2 (Chapter 5)"; the JAP article calls it Study 5.

The article (p. 788) points to "Table S10.1 in the SM"; the article's own supplement is not public. The thesis PDF is 167 MB and is kept out of git (`.gitignore`). It is image-only, so the English text below was transcribed from the page images and checked against them. The thesis also prints the Chinese version of each item; it is not transcribed here.

**Instruction** (article p. 785): "We will now present approximately 50 leadership descriptions… Please answer to what extent you perceive these leadership descriptions as effective if these individuals were your own leaders" (1 = not at all effective as a leader, 7 = extremely effective as a leader).

**Licence.** The thesis is CC BY-ND, so the items are reproduced verbatim, with source typos kept and marked [sic]. Each scale also carries its original authors' copyright. (R) marks a reverse-keyed item, as the thesis marks it. The number after each item is its CFA loading.

## Transformational leadership: Podsakoff et al. (1990); Wang & Howell (2010)

**Team-building** (α = .68)
1. Fosters collaboration among team members. .58
2. Encourages group members to be "team players." .64
3. Develops a team attitude and spirit among team members. .59
4. Gets the team to work together for the same goal. .49
5. Resolves friction among team members in the interest of teamwork. .44

**Vision communication** (α = .71)
1. Articulates a compelling vision of the future for the team. .67
2. Talks optimistically about the future of the team. .67
3. Talks enthusiastically about what needs to be accomplished by the team. .63
4. Communicates a clear direction of where the team is going. .50

**High-expectation** (α = .73)
1. Encourages subordinates to set high goals for themselves. .67
2. Communicates high performance expectations to subordinates. .62
3. Shows confidence in subordinates' ability to meet performance expectations. .57
4. Demonstrates total confidence in subordinates. .56
5. Encourages subordinates to live up to their potential. .55

**Intellectual stimulation** (α = .64)
1. Gets subordinates to look at problems from many different angles. .52
2. Challenges subordinates to think about old problems in new ways. .59
3. Challenges subordinates to be innovative in their approach to work assignments. .56
4. Encourages subordinates to be independent thinkers. .55

## Paternalistic leadership: Cheng et al. (2004, 2014)

**Authoritarianism** (α = .87)
1. Appears to be intimidating in front of staff of other departments/divisions. .80
2. Brings staff of other departments/divisions a lot of pressure when they work together. .73
3. Very strict with staff of other departments/divisions. .80
4. Scolds staff of other departments/divisions when they fail expected target. .72
5. Disciplines staff of other departments/divisions for violation of his/her principles. .76

**Benevolence** (α = .60)
1. Often shows his/her concern about subordinates. .49
2. Understands subordinates' preference enough to accommodate their personal requests. .47
3. Encourages subordinates when they encounter difficulties in work. .62
4. Would try to understand the real cause of subordinates' unsatisfied performance. .46
5. Trains and coaches subordinates when they lack required abilities at work. .38

**Moral character** (α = .63)
1. Avenges a personal wrong in the name of public interest when he/she is offended (R). .40
2. Employs people according to their virtues and does not envy others' abilities and virtues. .40
3. Uses his/her authority to seek special privileges for himself/herself (R). .46
4. Doesn't take the credit for subordinates' achievements and contributions for himself/herself. .45
5. Does not take advantage of subordinates for personal gain. .59
6. Does not use guanxi (personal relationships) or back-door practices to obtain illicit personal gains. .54

## Status attainment: Bai et al. (2020)

**Dominance** (α = .84)
1. Some subordinates are afraid of him/her. .82
2. Some subordinates are too scared to confront him/her. .73
3. Some subordinates dare not refuse his/her requests. .80
4. He/she enjoys having control over others. .67

**Competence** (α = .62)
1. His/her talents and abilities are recognized by others. .58
2. He/she possesses skills and expertise to succeed. .47
3. Subordinates respect his/her skills or expertise. .60
4. He/she is considered an expert on some matters by others. .47

**Virtue** (α = .70)
1. When subordinates think of him/her, they believe there is still some good in the world. .53
2. Subordinates are deeply touched by his/her goodness. .77
3. He/she helps subordinates recognize the better part of themselves. .47
4. He/she is willing to sacrifice his/her self-interest to help others. .43
5. Subordinates strive to follow his/her moral standards. .35
6. Subordinates feel uplifted by his/her goodness. .73

## Safety leadership: Hu et al. (2025)

**Safety** (α = .57)
1. Emphasizes work efficiency and also emphasizes work safety. .65
2. Meets the organization's need for revenue and also tells subordinates that the safety of employees and customers is the prerequisite for revenue. .49
3. Gives equal consideration to production goals and safe working. .53

## Narcissism: Back et al. (2013)

**Narcissism** (α = .71)
1. Thinks he/she deserves to be seen as a great personality. .72
2. Shows others how special he/her [sic] is. .70
3. Manages to be the center of attention with his/her outstanding contributions. .49
4. He/she thinks most people are somehow losers. .41
5. Wants his/her rivals to fail. .45
6. Reacts annoyed if another person steals the show from him/her. .40

## What these items change for our reading

- **Authoritarianism here faces outward.** All five items describe pressure on *staff of other departments/divisions*, not on one's own subordinates. The scale's target is the outgroup, which is the intergroup setting p. 789's question names. Protection still does not predict it (Model 2 β = .01).
- **Safety is occupational safety.** It means work safety balanced against production and revenue, not protection from a threat to the group. It has 3 items and the lowest α (.57). So protection → Safety (Model 1 β = .20, Model 2 β = .05) is weak evidence that protection reaches the effectiveness layer at all.
- **The dominance side has three different targets.** Dominance (Bai) is fear among one's own subordinates. Authoritarianism (Cheng) is toughness toward other units. Narcissism is self-aggrandisement. A single rating of one observed D persona cannot separate them.
- **Several scales have low α:** Safety .57, Benevolence .60, Competence .62, Moral character .63, Intellectual stimulation .64.
